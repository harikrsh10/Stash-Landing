What Stash does

More than a clipboard — a place things you copy actually stay useful

Eight ways Stash earns the shortcut you'll use fifty times a day.

drag

Drag straight into any app

Pull a clip out of Stash and drop it directly into VS Code, Figma, Notion — anywhere that accepts text or images. No copy-paste round-trip.

stack

Gather a stack, drop it in one go

⌘-click a handful of clips — screenshots, snippets, whatever — and drag the whole stack into Figma, an AI chat, or a folder. Everything lands at once.

prompts

Build a prompt library that doesn't age out

Mark a clip once and it's permanent — tagged, searchable, and never pushed out by the next hundred things you copy.

extract

Pull text out of any screenshot

Copy an image, hit extract, and pick out just the text you need — read by the same engine as macOS and Windows themselves, not a bundled guess.

colour

Lift the palette out of any image

Press colour on a screenshot or a photo and get what it's actually made of — every swatch with its hex, ready to copy. No eyedropper, no guessing at a gradient.

sessions

Keep each project's clips apart

Name a session, set it collecting, and everything you copy lands there instead of the general pile. Switch between them as you switch projects — each one keeps its own clips, across restarts.

secure

Skips secrets automatically

API keys, JWTs, and credit card numbers are detected and excluded from history before they're ever stored. Stops accidental leaks during screen-shares.

fast

One shortcut, gone in a flash

Cmd+Shift+V opens the drawer, picks the clip, closes itself. No window to manage, no app to alt-tab to, no flow broken.

Sessions

Three projects open at once, and none of their clips in each other's way

Most days aren't one job. A session is a named place for one project's references, copy and screenshots — so the moodboard you're building doesn't fill up with the deck you're also writing.

  1. 01

    Name a session for what you're working on

    One per client, per feature, per deck — however you already think about your work.

  2. 02

    Set it collecting, then just work

    Everything you copy lands in that session instead of the general pile. No filing, no dragging things into folders afterwards — you copy the way you already do.

  3. 03

    Switch projects, switch sessions

    Each one keeps its own clips — including its images — through quits and restarts. Come back next week and the references you gathered are still sitting together.

Security & privacy

Built to be trusted with what you copy

Stash runs entirely on your machine. Here's exactly what that means.

memory

Ordinary history never touches disk

What you copy lives in memory and clears the moment you quit. Only the clips you deliberately pin, and the prompts you save, are ever written down.

local

Nothing you copy ever leaves your device

Regular history lives in memory only and clears the moment you quit. Pinned clips and prompts are saved to one file on your own machine — never uploaded, never synced to a server.

filtered

Secrets never make it into history

API keys, JWTs, AWS credentials, 2FA codes and credit card numbers are detected and silently skipped before they're ever stored — so a screen-share can't leak what you just copied.

First run

From download to first paste, in about a minute

  1. 01

    Open the downloaded .dmg

    Double-click the file in your Downloads folder. When the window opens, drag the Stash icon into the Applications folder.

  2. 02

    Open Stash from Applications

    Double-click Stash to launch it. It appears in your menu bar and starts keeping your clipboard right away.

    Upgrading from an older build and using auto-paste? Remove Stash under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility and add it back.

  3. 03

    Press ⌘ ⇧ V to open Stash

    Stash lives in your menu bar. Use the shortcut from anywhere to slide the drawer in, then drag clips into your apps.

  1. 01

    Run Stash-win-x64.exe

    Double-click the installer in your Downloads folder. Windows will install Stash and add it to your Start menu.

  2. 02

    Get past the SmartScreen warning

    Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" prompt. Click More info, then Run anyway to continue.

    This happens because Stash isn't code-signed yet. The source is on GitHub if you want to inspect or build it yourself.

  3. 03

    Press Ctrl Shift V to open Stash

    Stash lives in your system tray. Use the shortcut from anywhere to slide the drawer in, then drag clips into your apps.
